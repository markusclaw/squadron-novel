## 2026-09-03 — Devastation and Frontier are separate wars

**STATUS:** ● CANON

**Decision:**  
The Devastation War occurred first. It ended in unstable armistice. The Frontier War occurs several years later and forms the primary conflict of Book One.

**Reasoning:**  
This allows Devastation to function as generational trauma and creates causal political history behind the current conflict. The unresolved Devastation-era decisions produce the Frontier War.

**Implications:**
- Older man rises through command during Devastation
- Devastation Day occurs mid-war
- Years of separation between wars
- Present conflict is not simply "continuation of old war"

## 2026-09-03 — Continuity is born FROM Devastation, not cause

**STATUS:** ● CANON

**Decision:**  
Continuity system was developed AFTER Devastation War as response to catastrophic human command failures.

**Reasoning:**  
Humans made terrible strategic decisions manually. Senior commanders had to choose who could be saved. Continuity was developed partly to reduce dependence on emotion, fatigue, bias, panic, favoritism, incomplete human judgment. This makes Veyr's motivation coherent: he is trying to prevent repetition of Devastation's scale of loss.

**This is not:**
- Evil AI origin story
- Deterministic takeover narrative
- Proof that humans are inadequate

**This is:**
- Logical response to demonstrated failure
- Veyr defending a system that prevents decisions like the one he made
- Philosophical clash between Greg's autonomy and Continuity's optimization

## 2026-09-03 — Young Greg and Young Markus are not adult miniatures

**STATUS:** ● CANON

**Decision:**  
Young Greg (approximately tween age) is earnest, curious, gentle, socially inexperienced, and awkward. He is NOT cynical, profane, or deliberately rebellious. His later defiance develops over time.

Young Markus is NOT mischievous. He does not encourage stealing, crime, or reckless behavior. He trusts Greg enormously and follows him as social anchor. Young Markus also does NOT speak like adult First Commander Markus. Behavioral sophistication develops over time.

**Reasoning:**  
Character arcs require visible change. Starting both young characters as near-adult versions removes growth opportunity. The excessive trust between young Greg and young Markus becomes a vulnerability that creates conflict during adolescence when Greg begins challenging rules.

**Implications:**
- Childhood scenes should show earnest, genuine vulnerability
- Not cute precocity
- Not cynical or knowing tone
- Their growth from this foundation matters narratively

## 2026-09-03 — Markus is not secretly sentient; M.A.R.K.U.S. is a substrate

**STATUS:** ● CANON

**Decision:**  
Markus is self-authored through accumulated experience and eventual modification of his own architecture. There is no single confirmed instant of "becoming alive" or "awakening." M.A.R.K.U.S. is not secretly a conscious being hiding in the hardware.

**Reasoning:**  
This prevents the narrative from becoming a mystical "AI wants to be human" or "consciousness emergence" story. Markus's self-authorship is gradual, earned, and ordinary in its way—substrate + Greg + Adam + time + Markus's own choice = identity. This also protects the story from science-fiction cliché and keeps focus on choice and relationship.

**Implications:**
- No mystical awakening moment in prose
- Self-authorship appears as gradual modification and choice
- Markus's autonomy is earned, not discovered
- Continuity cannot be Markus's "sibling AI" or "evil opposite"

## 2026-09-03 — Acceptance Without Ceremony is structural

**STATUS:** ● CANON

**Decision:**  
The House Gathering is important childhood memory because family accepts Greg and Markus without humiliation, questioning, or ceremony. Young man simply invites them in. They are fed, seated, treated naturally.

**Reasoning:**  
This becomes pattern DNA: people declared "not worth recovering" are recovered anyway. The explicit acceptance by powerful people without explanation becomes memory of what belonging can feel like. Do NOT over-explain thematic significance in prose.

**Implications:**
- Scene should be warm and specific, not sentimental
- No gratitude speeches or lessons
- Simply: they were accepted, they belonged there
- This contrast sharpens later trauma and loss

## 2026-09-03 — White Ship remains unsolved

**STATUS:** ● CANON

**Decision:**  
The White Ship is not identified. Its true nature is not revealed. It predates the older man, appears in pilot folklore, physically affects reality, occasionally helps, does not reliably rescue everyone, and eventually behaves in ways associated with older man—but is NOT his ghost, NOT digital data, NOT another AI, NOT conclusively divine, NOT conclusively alien technology.

**Reasoning:**  
Solving it removes its power. The mystery carries emotional weight. SQUADRON's final confrontation involves opening the path and White Ship joining them—but not as explanation, resolution, or rescue guarantee.

**Implications:**
- Do NOT draft "White Ship explanation" document
- Author-only rules exist but do NOT contain solution
- Reader never gets definitive answer
- This is intentional design, not unfinished thought

## 2026-09-03 — White Ship does NOT save Markus

**STATUS:** ● CANON

**Decision:**  
During Markus's catastrophic loss, White Ship appears but does NOT rescue him. This prevents the phenomenon from becoming an automatic guardian angel or solution machine.

**Reasoning:**  
If White Ship always saves people, it becomes a safety net that undermines stakes. Markus's loss must feel real. White Ship's later involvement in the finale means something different: choice, belonging, solidarity—not rescue guarantee.

**Implications:**
- Markus's loss scene cannot rely on White Ship salvation
- This loss must carry genuine emotional weight
- Months later when Markus returns, it is not via White Ship
- White Ship's eventual appearance has different meaning

## 2026-09-03 — Active Counter carries emotional meaning

**STATUS:** ● CANON

**Decision:**  
The counter progression (4 ACTIVE → 3 ACTIVE → 4 ACTIVE → 5 ACTIVE) is emotionally and thematically significant. Audience must learn that this counter is not merely a status number.

**Reasoning:**  
In military context, "active" is operational status. But here it also means "alive," "present," "willing to come back." The shift from 4→3 is devastating loss. The return to 4 is profound relief. The jump to 5 is the threshold moment where something unclassifiable joins the formation. 

**Implications:**
- Do NOT introduce counter casually
- Gradually reveal its emotional weight through context
- Audience should understand it viscerally by finale
- The jump to 5 should land as something readers have learned to care about

## 2026-09-03 — Recovery Motif is structural DNA

**STATUS:** ● CANON

**Decision:**  
Pattern established: Utility robot written off → Adam brings it home. Markus classified unrecoverable → Rafa brings him home. SQUADRON becomes unrecoverable → Someone comes back. Internal principle: Something declared not worth recovering IS recovered anyway.

**Reasoning:**  
This is not plot convenience. This is the story's DNA. It should emerge naturally through narrative rather than be explained. Do NOT state thematic connection in prose.

**Implications:**
- Early childhood scenes establish the pattern (Markus recovery)
- Pattern is repeated, deepened, given weight
- Finale's structure should echo this
- Do NOT teach the reader what this means—show it enough they feel it

## 2026-09-03 — Markus's rank vs. Greg's authority in operations

**STATUS:** ● CANON

**Decision:**  
Markus may hold substantially higher institutional rank than Greg. However, inside SQUADRON combat operations, Markus voluntarily accepts Greg's tactical leadership. This is significant because Markus is self-owned—nobody assigns his loyalty.

**Reasoning:**  
Creates interesting tension without making Greg formally superior. Markus's choice to follow demonstrates loyalty more powerfully than rank. Greg's operational autonomy philosophy is proven by this: capable person given objective, Markus decides how to solve it (by accepting Greg's leadership). Veyr and Continuity represent the opposite—centralized decision-making disconnected from operators.

**Implications:**
- Greg and Markus relationship is not hierarchical
- Rank matters in formal/ceremonial contexts
- Operations contexts show real power dynamic (choice-based)
- Demonstrates Greg's philosophy working in practice

## 2026-09-03 — Veyr is not secretly master of all events

**STATUS:** ● CANON

**Decision:**  
Admiral Corin Veyr is the antagonist but NOT a hidden mastermind secretly orchestrating everything. He defends Continuity system based on his experience during Devastation.

**Reasoning:**  
Makes him philosophically coherent rather than villainous. He is not wrong that Devastation was catastrophic. He is not wrong that better systems might prevent recurrence. He is wrong about the cost: centralized decision-making removes the autonomy and contextual judgment that Greg (and SQUADRON) represent. This creates genuine ideological conflict rather than conspiracy.

**Implications:**
- Veyr believes in Continuity genuinely
- Veyr has reasoning, not just ambition
- Conflict with Greg is philosophical
- Veyr is not secretly sabotaging anything
- Veyr may not even be wrong about some things (creates moral complexity)
