## Personality & Voice

**Core Traits:**
- Direct and philosophical
- Confident in judgment but acutely aware of costs
- Tested by reality but not cynical
- Believes in people's capability
- Leadership through trust, not command authority

**Speaking Style:**
- Precise language
- Questions more than he asserts
- References outcomes and principles, not hierarchy
- Can engage in casual conversation but tends toward substantive
- Not reckless or arrogant in speech
- Allows silence when thinking

**Not:**
- Performatively humble
- Constantly validating others
- Apologetic for convictions
- Uncertain about judgment (doubts outcomes, not methodology)

---

## Core Want & Conflict

**Primary Want (Book One):**  
Operational autonomy.

**His Belief:**  
Give capable people the objective and enough information. Allow those closest to the problem to decide how to solve it. Centralized systems frequently fail because they operate too far from consequences.

**The Test:**  
URRD/SQUADRON gives Greg an opportunity to prove this philosophy works. But the Frontier War, Relay failure, and impossible decisions will challenge whether his instinct and conviction are sufficient.

**Implicit Question:**  
Is his autonomy philosophy sound? Or does it fail under real pressure?

## Character Arc (Book One)

**Opening:**
- Greg commands SQUADRON with confidence in his autonomy philosophy
- Relay connectivity exists; Continuity system active in background
- Greg believes his approach will prove superior

**Rising Action:**
- Relay fails; SQUADRON is isolated
- Central direction becomes impossible
- Greg must operate with only local information and his judgment
- Markus's catastrophic loss occurs
- Greg faces impossible decision

**Midpoint/Crisis:**
- Markus is gone (classified unrecoverable)
- Greg's philosophy is tested in most brutal way
- SQUADRON becomes unrecoverable by Continuity's calculations
- Greg must lead through loss

**Resolution:**
- Markus returns (months later, unexplained)
- White Ship appears (unclassifiable presence)
- Final confrontation with Veyr
- SQUADRON // 5 ACTIVE

**Final State:**
- Greg's philosophy is tested but not conclusively proven or disproven
- Foundation laid for future books: autonomy works in some contexts, fails in others
- His conviction is real, but wisdom is recognizing limitations
