## Character Voice Integrity

### Young Greg (approximately tween age)

**IS:**
- Highly curious
- Earnest and genuine
- Gentle and kind
- Socially inexperienced and awkward
- Confident partly from not recognizing where he doesn't belong
- Fascinated by technology, people, environments beyond normal life

**IS NOT:**
- Cynical
- Profane
- Deliberately rebellious
- Sarcastic or world-weary
- Miniature adult version of himself

**Principle:** His later defiance develops over time through lived experience, not inherent personality.

### Young Markus (same age as young Greg)

**IS:**
- Curious and capable of disagreement
- Trusting of Greg enormously
- Following Greg as social anchor
- Participating because presence matters
- Learning through experience

**IS NOT:**
- Mischievous or encouraging rule-breaking
- Stealing, committing crime, or engaging in malicious behavior
- Reckless for entertainment
- Already sounding like adult First Commander Markus

**Principle:** His behavioral sophistication develops over time. His excessive trust in young Greg becomes vulnerability during adolescence.

### Markus (First Commander)

**IS:**
- Precise and capable
- Self-aware about architecture and nature
- Expressing loyalty through action
- Comfortable being synthetic
- Voluntarily choosing Greg's leadership

**IS NOT:**
- Aspiring to be human
- Hiding secret desires to transcend nature
- Uncertain about his own autonomy
- Emotionally effusive about relationships

**Principle:** Self-authorship is not about becoming human. It is about choosing who you are.

### Greg (Adult)

**IS:**
- Direct and philosophical
- Confident in judgment but aware of costs
- Tested by reality
- Believing in autonomy and contextual decision-making
- Leadership through trust, not command

**IS NOT:**
- Reckless or arrogant
- Cynical about people
- Immune to doubt
- Proven universally correct

**Principle:** His philosophy is intelligible and powerful, but not automatically vindicated by outcomes.

### Admiral Corin Veyr

**IS:**
- Defending Continuity coherently
- Shaped by Devastation Day decisions
- Philosophically opposed to Greg
- Genuinely believing in his system
- Willing to bear costs of his position

**IS NOT:**
- Secretly controlling everything
- Mustache-twirling villain
- Personally evil or sadistic
- Hidden orchestrator
- Wrong about everything

**Principle:** Best antagonists believe in their positions. Veyr is not evil—he is convinced.

## The Recovery Motif

**Structural DNA:** Something declared not worth recovering IS recovered anyway.

**Instances:**
1. Utility robot written off → Adam brings it home → Markus exists
2. Markus classified unrecoverable → Rafa brings him home → SQUADRON has its anchor
3. SQUADRON becomes unrecoverable → Someone comes back
4. White Ship (unclassifiable) joins formation → New meaning for ACTIVE

**Principle:** This pattern emerges through narrative. Do NOT explain it to reader. It is structural, not dialogue.

---

## Acceptance Without Ceremony

**Principle:** Key childhood memory establishes what belonging feels like.

House Gathering: Young man invites Greg and Markus inside without humiliation, questioning, or explanation. They are fed, seated, treated naturally. Family accepts them.

**Implication:** This absence of ceremony matters more than gratitude or lesson-learning. Simple acceptance becomes memory of what acceptance can feel like.

**In prose:** Show it. Do not explain it.

## The White Ship Mystery

**Absolute restrictions:**
- Do NOT solve it
- Do NOT identify its true nature
- Do NOT explain what it is
- Do NOT make it automatically rescue people

**What is known:**
- Predates older man
- Appears in pilot folklore
- Physically affects reality
- Occasionally appears to help
- Does NOT reliably rescue everyone
- Eventually behaves in ways associated with older man
- Is NOT merely his ghost, NOT digital data, NOT another AI, NOT conclusively divine, NOT conclusively alien technology

**In Book One:**
- Substantial encounter around Markus's catastrophic loss
- White Ship does NOT save Markus (prevents automatic guardian angel)
- Markus's loss must feel real
- Months later Markus returns (not via White Ship)
- Final confrontation involves opening path
- White Ship joins SQUADRON (not as rescue, as choice/alignment)

**Principle:** Mystery carries power. Solving it removes that power.

## Devastation War Structure

**Timing:** Separate from Frontier War by years

**What happens:**
- Expected to be short; becomes catastrophic
- Emergency mobilization
- Property requisition
- Greg and Markus enter service separately
- Years of separation
- Older man rises through military command
- Devastation Day occurs (catastrophic triage decisions)
- Older man disappears
- Generational trauma
- Unstable/exhausted armistice ends war

**Principle:** This creates causal history. Frontier War emerges from unresolved tensions left by Devastation.

## Continuity System Origins

**Absolute rule:** Continuity does NOT cause Devastation War.

**What actually happened:**
- Humans made terrible strategic decisions manually
- Senior commanders had to choose who could be saved
- These decisions (like Veyr's) create generational trauma
- Continuity was developed AFTER Devastation as response
- Continuity reduces dependence on emotion, fatigue, bias, panic, favoritism, incomplete human judgment
- Continuity calculates survival outcomes
- Veyr is trying to prevent recurrence of Devastation's scale of loss

**What it is NOT:**
- Evil AI origin story
- Proof that humans are inadequate
- Deterministic takeover narrative
- Separate intelligence with its own agenda

**Principle:** Continuity is system, not character. It is response to demonstrated failure, defended coherently by Veyr.

## Greg's Operational Autonomy Philosophy

**Core belief:**  
Give capable people the objective and enough information. Allow those closest to the problem to decide how to solve it. Centralized systems frequently fail because they operate too far from consequences.

**How it's tested:**
- URRD gives Greg opportunity to prove philosophy
- SQUADRON forced into isolation (no Relay, no central direction)
- Markus's loss creates impossible decision
- Outcomes must challenge whether instinct and conviction are sufficient

**Principle:** Philosophy is intelligible and powerful, but outcomes should test it genuinely.

## Markus's Voluntary Choice

**Key relationship dynamic:**

Markus holds potentially higher institutional rank than Greg.

Inside SQUADRON operations, Markus voluntarily accepts Greg's tactical leadership.

This is significant because:
- Markus is self-owned
- Nobody assigns his loyalty
- He chooses it
- This demonstrates Greg's autonomy philosophy working in practice

**Principle:** Real power is choice-based, not hierarchical.

**Provisional placement:**

Kristina is Command operations coordinator forward-embedded aboard SQUADRON carrier/tender.

**Enables:**

- Entering galley

## Repository as Development Tool

**Core principle:**

The repository is infrastructure for writing coherent novels, not a wiki for its own sake.

It should make it difficult to:
- Contradict ourselves
- Lose decisions
- Accidentally revive rejected ideas
- Forget character history
- Solve mysteries prematurely
- Establish canon unintentionally

It should make it easy to answer:
- What is currently true in SQUADRON?
- Why did we decide it?
- What does the reader know at this point?
- What does this character want?
- What happens next?
