# The House Gathering

A defining childhood memory — **acceptance without ceremony.**

## The canon <span class="sq-badge canon">CANON</span>

Greg and Markus are roughly 10–12. They have spent the day around a nearby stream and
are dirty. Greg has an old, peeling red bicycle. Passing the [House on the Hill](../history/house_on_the_hill.md),
they see numerous expensive vehicles, elegant guests, military personnel, political and
social figures, music, food, firelight — a formal but warm gathering (likely
celebrating the [older man](../characters/older_man.md)'s academy graduation and command
appointment, which the boys don't fully understand).

They wander near the property. The older man sees them and simply invites them inside.
**Nobody humiliates them. Nobody questions why they are there. Nobody treats Markus as
an object of ridicule.** They are fed; they sit among adults; they miss most social
cues. Greg listens to conversations he barely understands. Markus imitates eating
because participation matters to him.

Greg is fascinated by the technology, the vehicles, the clothes, the social ease, how
adults speak, how comfortable everyone seems. The family's acceptance is never
explained — it is simply demonstrated.
