# Themes

<span class="sq-status">Status: <span class="sq-badge provisional">PROVISIONAL</span> — the through-lines below are strongly implied by the established canon.</span>

The book is *about* a set of questions beneath the plot. We hold the questions, not
the answers — different characters embody different answers, and the story tests them.

## Belonging and classification

Who counts as one of us, and who decides? Markus is a person humanity once struggled
to *classify* — a written-off machine that became someone. The book's turn is that
the once-unclassifiable person becomes the one willing to classify an unclassifiable
presence as **FRIENDLY**. *(Do not state this connection in prose.)*

## Recovery

*Something declared not worth recovering is recovered anyway.* The written-off robot;
Markus classified unrecoverable; the squadron itself. This is **structural DNA, not
dialogue** — it is never named to the reader.

## Autonomy versus calculation

Greg's belief that judgment belongs closest to consequence, against Continuity's
belief that survival is too important to leave to exhausted, biased human minds.
Both save lives. Both are sometimes right.

## Acceptance without ceremony

The House on the Hill memory: welcome that is *demonstrated, never explained.*
Warmth that asks for no justification. *(Do not over-explain its significance in prose.)*

## The cost carried by those who decide

Veyr, who lives with a triage decision that sacrificed a smaller force to prevent a
greater catastrophe. Command as the weight of impossible calculations — the very
weight Continuity is meant to lift from human shoulders.

## Chosen loyalty

Markus is self-owned; nobody assigns his loyalty. Inside SQUADRON he *chooses* to
follow Greg's tactical lead. A squadron is defined by who chooses to come back.

    event* in a scene rather than explaining the theme in narration or dialogue.
