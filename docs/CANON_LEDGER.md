Legend: `[CANON]` accepted · `[PROVISIONAL]` preferred but changeable · `[OPEN]` undecided · `[REJECTED]` abandoned.

## Markus

- [CANON] Digital-born synthetic person.
- [CANON] Originated from a damaged, economically written-off compact industrial utility robot.
- [CANON] Original proprietary manufacturer software was wiped before the hardware left the facility.
- [CANON] Adam installed a saved legacy runtime, **M.A.R.K.U.S.**, onto the hardware.
- [CANON] The framework identified itself as M.A.R.K.U.S.; Greg heard "Markus"; the designation became a name.
- [CANON] Greg grew up alongside Markus; Markus accumulated persistent experience over years.
- [CANON] Markus physically grew through progressively taller, more capable bodies.
- [CANON] Markus eventually began modifying the M.A.R.K.U.S. architecture itself — the threshold of self-authorship.
- [CANON] Markus is self-authored: he owns his cognition, memory, and identity.
- [CANON] Markus does **not** aspire to become human.
- [CANON] There is no single confirmed instant where Markus "became alive."
- [PROVISIONAL] M.A.R.K.U.S. = "Modular Adaptive Runtime Kernel for Utility Systems."
- [PROVISIONAL] Military rank title: First Commander.
- [PROVISIONAL] Markus may hold a substantially higher institutional rank than Greg, yet voluntarily accepts Greg's tactical leadership inside SQUADRON operations.

## Greg

- [CANON] Human. Grew up on a far-future human colony world (not Earth) alongside Markus.
- [CANON] As a child (tween-aged, ~10–12 in the childhood material): curious, earnest, gentle, socially inexperienced, awkward, unaware of where he doesn't belong. Not cynical, not profane, not deliberately rebellious.
- [CANON] Fascinated by technology, people, and environments beyond his ordinary life.
- [CANON] Book One want: **operational autonomy** — give capable people the objective and enough information, and let those closest to the problem decide how to solve it.
- [PROVISIONAL] URRD gives Greg the opportunity to prove this philosophy; his arc tests whether instinct and conviction are enough.
- [REJECTED] Childhood Greg written as adult Greg in miniature.

## Adam

- [CANON] Greg's father. Works at a major industrial/logistics facility supporting the colony.
- [CANON] Brought the damaged utility robot home; found and adapted the legacy M.A.R.K.U.S. package to it.
- [CANON] Repaired Markus's hardware over years.

## The Older Man (House on the Hill)

- [CANON] Several years older than Greg; lives with an influential family on a large property near the town.
- [CANON] Gracious; treats Greg and Markus naturally. Technically gifted, mechanically intuitive.
- [CANON] Later helped improve Markus's legs, balance geometry, and servos.
- [CANON] Graduated an elite military academy; obtained a prestigious command; became a significant squadron commander.
- [CANON] Disappeared during Devastation Day; later appears only ambiguously in official memorial records.
- [PROVISIONAL] The family's father holds an important colonial-development / industrial / strategic role.
- [REJECTED] Described as Greg's "best friend." (Greg is a child who admires him; Greg likely overestimates the closeness.)
- [REJECTED] The family as a secret conspiracy family.

## Veyr (antagonist)

- [PROVISIONAL] Name: Admiral Corin Veyr.
- [PROVISIONAL] Role strongly preferred: senior commander during the Devastation War who participated in or authorized a terrible triage decision on Devastation Day.
- [PROVISIONAL] That decision sacrificed a smaller force and prevented a much greater catastrophe; the older man's formation may have been among those classified unrecoverable.
- [PROVISIONAL] Veyr defends Continuity as a way to prevent civilization depending on one exhausted human making impossible calculations.
- [REJECTED] Veyr as an evil mastermind who secretly controls everything.

## Kristina

- [PROVISIONAL] Command operations coordinator, forward-embedded aboard the SQUADRON detachment's carrier/tender.
- [PROVISIONAL] When the squadron loses Relay connectivity, Kristina disappears from their communications — making Relay failure emotionally tangible.

## The Wars

- [PROVISIONAL] The Devastation War and the Frontier War are **separate** conflicts. Devastation came first and ended in an unstable/exhausted armistice; the Frontier War comes years later and is Book One's primary conflict. (→ CANON once chronology is finalized.)
- [REJECTED] The present conflict and the Devastation War being the same war.

## Continuity

- [PROVISIONAL] A survival-outcome calculation system, developed **after** and **because of** Devastation, to reduce dependence on exhausted, biased, panicked human judgment.
- [CANON] Continuity did **not** cause Devastation Day.
- [REJECTED] Continuity as "evil AI." It can be correct; it genuinely saves lives; it is not equivalent to Markus.

## The White Ship

- [CANON] Its true nature is **OPEN by authorial rule** — it is not to be solved.
- [CANON] It predates the older man; appears in historical pilot folklore; appears around unrecoverable situations; physically affects reality; does not reliably rescue everyone.
- [CANON] It does **not** save Markus during his catastrophic loss.
- [REJECTED] Conclusively: his ghost · digital data · another AI · divine · alien technology.
- [PROVISIONAL] Eventually behaves in ways associated with the older man (but is not merely him).
- [PROVISIONAL] Finale: SQUADRON opens the path; the White Ship joins them. Markus classifies the unknown contact as FRIENDLY → SQUADRON // 5 ACTIVE.

## Structural DNA

- [CANON] Recovery motif (structural, never stated to the reader): *something declared not worth recovering is recovered anyway.* Robot written off → Adam brings it home. Markus classified unrecoverable → Rafa brings him home. SQUADRON unrecoverable → someone comes back.
- [CANON] The Active Counter (4 → 3 → 4 → 5) carries accumulating emotional meaning.
