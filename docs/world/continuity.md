# Continuity

<span class="sq-status">Status: <span class="sq-badge provisional">PROVISIONAL</span> (preferred direction) — with hard <span class="sq-badge rejected">REJECTED</span> boundaries</span>

The philosophical opposition of Book One. Continuity is a system, and an idea, that
stands against Greg's belief in operational autonomy — **not** a villain.

## What Continuity is <span class="sq-badge provisional">PROVISIONAL</span>

A survival-outcome calculation system. It calculates who can be saved. It was
developed **after**, and **because of**, the [Devastation War](devastation_war.md), to
reduce civilization's dependence on:

- emotion
- fatigue
- bias
- panic
- favoritism
- incomplete human judgment

## How it must be written

- It **can be correct.** It genuinely saves lives.
- It opposes Greg **philosophically** — centralized calculation against judgment made
  closest to consequence.

!!! danger "Hard boundaries"
    - Continuity did **not** cause Devastation Day. <span class="sq-badge canon">CANON</span>
      **Continuity is born *from* Devastation**, not the reverse.
    - It is **not** evil AI. <span class="sq-badge rejected">REJECTED</span>
    - It is **not** equivalent to [Markus](../characters/markus.md). A self-authored
      person and a survival calculator are different kinds of thing, and the story must
      never blur them.

## Its human advocate

[Admiral Corin Veyr](../characters/veyr.md) defends Continuity — to him it is the way
to prevent civilization from ever again depending on one exhausted human making
impossible calculations, as he had to on Devastation Day.

## The argument it anchors

Greg: judgment belongs closest to consequence. Continuity: survival is too important to
leave to exhausted, biased minds. The book keeps the argument genuinely open — both
sides are sometimes right.
