# Admiral Corin Veyr

<span class="sq-status">Role: antagonist (role strongly preferred) · Name: <span class="sq-badge provisional">PROVISIONAL</span></span>

## Snapshot

The book's antagonist — philosophical, not villainous. A senior commander during the
Devastation War who carries the weight of an impossible decision, and who sees in
**Continuity** a way to spare future commanders that weight.

## Established direction <span class="sq-badge provisional">PROVISIONAL</span>

- Name: **Admiral Corin Veyr** (provisional). Role: strongly preferred.
- Was a senior commander during the Devastation War.
- Personally participated in or authorized a terrible **triage decision on
  Devastation Day** that sacrificed a smaller force and prevented a much greater
  catastrophe.
- **The older man's formation may have been among those classified unrecoverable.**
- He lives with the decision.
- To him, Continuity is a way to prevent civilization from depending on one exhausted
  human making impossible calculations.

## How he must be written

- He should **genuinely save lives** and be **correct sometimes.**
- He opposes Greg **philosophically** — centralized calculation vs. operational autonomy.
- <span class="sq-badge rejected">REJECTED</span> He does **not** secretly control
  everything and does **not** behave as an evil mastermind. He *defends* Continuity.

## Function in the argument

Veyr is the human face of Continuity's case. His grief makes the case sympathetic;
his correctness makes it dangerous to Greg's conviction. The story's central
argument — autonomy vs. calculation — only works if Veyr is right often enough to
matter.
