# House on the Hill

<span class="sq-status">Status: <span class="sq-badge canon">CANON</span> (core), with the father's role provisional</span>

An influential family lives on a large property near the town. For young Greg, the
House on the Hill is a window into a world of technology, ease, and adult
self-assurance — and the home of the older man he admires.

## The older man <span class="sq-badge canon">CANON</span>

Several years older than Greg. Do **not** currently describe him as Greg's "best
friend" <span class="sq-badge rejected">REJECTED</span> — Greg is a child who strongly
admires him and likely **overestimates** the closeness of the relationship. This is
intentional.

He is genuinely gracious and treats Greg and Markus naturally. Technically gifted and
mechanically intuitive, casually welcoming, he encourages young Greg and treats Markus
as a matter of course. He later helps improve Markus's physical hardware; graduates
from an elite military academy; obtains a prestigious command appointment; eventually
becomes a significant squadron commander; disappears during Devastation Day; and later
appears only ambiguously in official memorial records. See
[The Older Man](../characters/older_man.md).

## The family <span class="sq-badge canon">CANON</span> / <span class="sq-badge provisional">PROVISIONAL</span>

Wealthy, successful, politically and socially connected, accustomed to formal social
settings, connected to military/government/industrial circles.

- <span class="sq-badge rejected">REJECTED</span> They are **not** a secret conspiracy
  family.
- <span class="sq-badge provisional">PROVISIONAL</span> The father is in the town for
  an important colonial-development / industrial / strategic role. Exact role remains
  provisional.

## Why it matters

The House on the Hill is where Greg first sees the world he will later move through —
command, technology, social ease — and where he learns, through the
[House Gathering](../scenes/house_gathering.md), what **acceptance without ceremony**
feels like. The family's warmth is never explained; it is simply demonstrated.
